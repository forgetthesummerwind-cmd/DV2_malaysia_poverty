Malaysia's Hidden
Living Pressure

Poverty. Income. Household strain.

Malaysia's national progress is real, but it is not evenly experienced. This data story follows the pressure from where poverty concentrates, through the different forms it takes, to the income and expenditure forces that keep some households vulnerable.

01

Geographic lens

Pressure is regional

State-level patterns reveal disadvantage that a national average conceals.

02

Poverty lens

One rate is not enough

Broad deprivation, severe hardship and relative disadvantage do not identify the same places.

03

Household lens

Income can mislead

Higher earnings do not automatically remove inequality or expenditure pressure.

1

The geographic divide

Poverty is concentrated, not evenly shared

We begin with place. The national rate becomes more meaningful when Malaysia is viewed state by state and within its Southeast Asian context.

Figure 01 · Choropleth map

Sabah is the clearest absolute-poverty hotspot

Deep blue encodes deprivation, while surrounding countries remain neutral. The spatial gap shows why a single national average cannot describe Malaysia's lived reality.

Map insightSabah records 19.7% absolute poverty in 2022, far above the lowest-rate federal territories.

Source: Poverty by State, Malaysia, 2022. Malaysia state GeoJSON and Vega world map data.

Figure 02 · Ranked bar chart

The state ranking exposes the size of the regional gap

The map locates the problem; the ordered bars make its magnitude precise. Sabah, Kelantan and Sarawak form a visibly different poverty tier.

Sabah, Kelantan and Sarawak form the top poverty tier.

Source: Poverty by State, Malaysia, 2022.

Why it matters

Geography identifies where pressure concentrates, but not what kind of hardship households experience.

That requires separating poverty into its different dimensions.
2

The poverty divide

Poverty is not a single number

Absolute, hardcore and relative poverty reveal different forms of hardship across the same states.

Figure 03 · Lollipop plot

Severe hardship is smaller in scale, but sharply concentrated

Because hardcore poverty values are low, a lollipop plot preserves small but meaningful differences.

Key reading

Sabah records the highest hardcore poverty rate at 1.2%.

Source: Poverty by State, Malaysia, 2022.

Figure 04 · Parallel coordinates

Different states carry different poverty profiles

Parallel axes compare how each state's exposure changes across relative, absolute and hardcore poverty.

Key reading

Sabah reaches the dimension maximum for absolute and hardcore poverty.

Values are normalised within each poverty dimension. 100% means the highest state in that dimension, not a 100% poverty rate.

Source: Poverty by State, Malaysia, 2022.

Main pattern

A state can rank lower on basic deprivation yet remain highly exposed to relative disadvantage.

The next question is whether income growth has reduced these differences equally.
3

The income divide

Income growth has not produced equal security

Household incomes have risen over time, but outcomes still diverge substantially across states.

Figure 05 · Multi-series line chart

Income rose across Malaysia, but the distance between states persisted

Selected states keep the long-run trend readable while showing how high-income urban regions pulled away from Sabah, Kelantan and Sarawak.

Source: Household Income by State, Malaysia, 1970–2022.

Figure 06 · Dumbbell chart

Mean income often sits well above the typical household

The distance between median and mean income shows how strongly high earners lift the average.

Key reading

Putrajaya has the widest gap between mean and median income.

Source: Household Income by State, Malaysia, 2022.

Figure 07 · Scatter plot with regression

Higher income usually lowers poverty, but not perfectly

The downward trend is clear, yet several states sit away from the expected line. Income alone cannot explain every poverty outcome.

Key reading

Sabah remains a high-poverty outlier despite its higher median income.

Source: Household Income and Poverty by State, Malaysia, 2022.

Figure 08 · Bump chart

Poverty rankings move, but regional vulnerability remains persistent

Tracking rank shows which states remain near the top of the poverty hierarchy across survey years.

Note: This is a ranking chart, not a poverty-rate chart. Rank 1 is placed at the top because it represents the state with the highest poverty rate. Lines that remain near the top indicate states that consistently experience higher poverty vulnerability across survey years.

Source: Poverty by State, Malaysia, 2012–2022.

Why it matters

Income explains part of the story, but household security depends on what income must cover.

The final chapter introduces expenditure and the resulting living-pressure ratio.
4

The household pressure divide

Living pressure appears when expenditure meets income

Combining expenditure with income identifies states where apparently adequate earnings still leave little room.

Figure 09 · Heatmap

Household expenditure rose in available post-2019 survey years

The matrix compares states across available survey years. Deeper gold-brown indicates higher monthly expenditure.

Source: Household Expenditure by State, Malaysia, 2019–2024.

Figure 10 · Quadrant bubble plot

High income does not guarantee low household pressure

Income sets the horizontal position and expenditure sets the vertical position. Bubble size shows absolute poverty, while darker colour indicates a higher expenditure-to-income ratio. This helps show that high-income states may still face high living costs, while lower-income states may carry stronger poverty vulnerability.

Note: A higher expenditure-to-income ratio means households spend a larger share of income, leaving less financial breathing room. A ratio close to 1.0 means monthly expenditure is close to monthly income. Bubble size should be read together with colour: large bubbles show poverty severity, while darker colour shows higher spending pressure.

The big picture

Malaysia's living pressure is spatial, multidimensional and household-specific.

  • Sabah, Kelantan and Sarawak carry the clearest absolute-poverty burden.
  • Hardcore and relative poverty reveal different vulnerabilities from absolute poverty.
  • Income growth has not erased the distance between states or the gap between mean and median households.
  • Expenditure changes the interpretation of wellbeing: earnings matter, but so does how much of them households must spend.
